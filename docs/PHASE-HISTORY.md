# stratus — phase history

> Full chronological ledger of every shipped phase/overhaul, moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md` (2026-07-23) to keep session context lean. **Docs-sync tasks append their phase entry HERE, not in CLAUDE.md.** Each entry is the authoritative "what shipped and why" record for its phase; the codemap (`.claude/skills/plan-feature/references/codemap.md`) is the current-state index.


- **XR — the X ranker port: X's published For You weights as a second, measurable signal (2026-09-02, `plans/2026-09-02-x-ranker-port.md`, 8 tasks, $0):** goal 2 primarily, goal 1 secondarily. The Composer already graded a draft with `postCoach` — 29 prose and hygiene rules whose own header concedes that writing quality is nearly uncorrelated with impressions. What it could not say is what the *ranker* is predisposed to do with the shape in the box. X publishes the production weights (`xai-org/x-algorithm`, `home-mixer/params/param.rs`, synced from production by cron), so the honest answer was to transcribe them and run the arithmetic ourselves. Three surfaces came out of it: a **C** pill beside the coach pill (prospective — the weights over probabilities estimated from our own signals), an **E** chip and badge on Radar sightings and on the on-page swipe-file save (retrospective — the same weights over a post's measured rates), and the Playbook cell that asks whether either number means anything on our corpus.

  **Two pure modules, and the split between them is the design.** `src/shared/xRanker.ts` holds facts and nothing else — `X_HEADS` (26 heads, each carrying its `param.rs` name, its label, and whether a $0 DOM harvest can observe it), `X_WEIGHT_SUMS` built exactly as `ScoringWeights::new` builds them, `offsetScore` / `scoreHeads` / `replyWeightFor` / `oonApplies` / `diversityMultiplier` / `normalizeScore`. `src/shared/xRankerSignals.ts` holds guesses — `X_BASELINE_P` (signal-free priors), `X_MODIFIERS` (23 entries, every one naming a source in **our** vocabulary: a `postCoach` check id, a `postFormat` label, or a `DraftFeatures` flag), `scoreDraftRanker` (C) and `scoreMeasured` (E). Facts and estimates never share a file, precisely so nobody later reads a modifier magnitude as a published parameter. Both are zero-dependency siblings of `replyBand`/`postCoach`/`searchQuery`, reached from the panel and the content script through bare re-export shims (`tsconfig.app.json` `include` 12 → **14** shims).

  **Attribution is per FILE and per READ, and the two headers deliberately disagree.** The feature was inspired by Bangermeter (MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Ryan Lenk), but the Read-first paths for XR.1 were absent from the machine when it ran, so the weight layer was transcribed **one layer upstream, from X's own repository** (`xai-org/x-algorithm` @ `7ba77684`, Apache-2.0) — and `xRanker.ts` credits xAI with the commit and the retrieval date, naming Bangermeter truthfully as the prior art. The tree reappeared before XR.2, whose material (the baseline priors, the shrinkage, the head split) *was* read there at first hand, so `xRankerSignals.ts` carries the MIT notice. A citation is a factual claim like any other: it records what its author actually opened, not what the project has on disk today.

  **Every borrowed number was checked against our own corpus before it shipped, and two of the three checks changed what shipped.** `X_OBSERVED_RATES` — E's reference for "what a typical post does per view" — is **measured**: favorite `0.029412`, reply `0.019774`, retweet **`0`**, over n=766 passive-timeline rows from 2026-07-24..2026-08-17, replacing the source's 158-post sample. Retweet zero is the honest median, not a gap: 66% of the posts our feed shows have no reposts at all. **Our feed is also not a general X feed** and every number in the lane inherits that — median 297 views against Bangermeter's 6,600, median reply rate 14.6x theirs — which is exactly why it is the right reference for scoring *our* sightings and the wrong one to quote as a fact about X. The borrowed 40/65 band cuts turned out to be a **dead instrument reading as a working one**: on 308 own published originals C read `below` 0.3% of the time, and on 3,567 harvested sightings E read `strong` 0.1% — a dead band at each end, at *opposite* ends, because one function was serving two distributions. They ship as two measured pairs, each the q1/q3 of the corpus its scale is applied to, so a band word now means a quartile position; and `rankerBand` became `rankerDraftBand` / `rankerMeasuredBand`, two functions rather than one with a flag, because a wrong flag there is silent (61 reads `typical` on one scale and `strong` on the other). The third check *closed* a question instead of licensing a change: the shrinkage constant K was suspected of flattening E, and method-of-moments over the same corpus put it within noise of where it already was — K stays at 2000, on evidence.

  **The falsification cell shipped, is correct, and reads empty — those are two separate facts.** `GET /x/playbook` returns `rankerScoreEffectiveness`: own harvested originals cut into quartiles by E and, over the same rows, by C, each cell gated at the operator's `Playbook cell gate`, with a spread that names the two gated quartiles it divided. Its population is `harvest_rows mode='posts'` for my own handle — **never** `metrics_snapshots`, which has been frozen since 2026-08-12 and carries no reply or repost counts anyway. On prod that population is **zero rows**: no `posts`-mode harvest of my own profile has ever been run, and the 2,537 rows under that mode are nineteen other people's profiles. So the cell reports the empty reading rather than being widened to a population that would answer a different question, and it was proven correct against a scratch DB pointed at a real 481-post profile harvest — C spread **1.71x** (q4 median 3,256 views against q1's 1,905) and E spread **0.50x**, E backwards exactly as its own header says it must be, because views is E's denominator. **C is the non-circular test and it has no verdict yet.** No panel table renders any of this: until the cell says something there is nothing to render, and `scripts/smoke-x-ranker.ts` prints it with one verdict line on every run.

  **What the user sees.** In the Composer, a `C 67 · typical` pill in the coach's head row, computed off the same debounced string and the same coach result the score pill renders — two readings of one evaluation, never two evaluations of one draft. It borrows **no** coach colour class: two engines on one ramp is how a reader starts averaging two numbers that answer different questions, so the pill is a `chips.ts` chip and only `below typical` takes a tone. In the Radar queue, an `E 52 · strong shape` chip on any sighting that carried a view count — scoring **the post, never the drafted reply**, because X drops out-of-network replies from For You outright. On x.com, an `E 47` badge leading the action cluster the **Save to stratus** button already owns, four glyphs wide because X's action row has no slack, repainted per scan and keyed on the tweet id (X recycles action rows as it virtualises the timeline, and a stamped-once score would follow a recycled row onto somebody else's post). Everywhere, an absent input is **absent, not zero**: no view count means no chip, no badge, no `ranker_e`.

  **Migration `0032_ordinary_iron_lad`** puts five nullable columns on `voice_tweets` (`views`/`likes`/`replies`/`reposts` + `ranker_e`), so a hunted tweet keeps the numbers it was hunted for. The client reports the counts and **the server scores them** — a `rankerE` on the wire is ignored, because it is a number this service could not re-derive or defend — and the five move as **one observation**, together or not at all: the per-column `?? existing` idiom two lines above would leave a row whose score is not the score of its own columns, breaking the identity (`ranker_e === scoreMeasured(row)`) every later analysis assumes.

  **Two silent `extractArticle` bugs were fixed in passing, and both were poisoning data rather than crashing anything.** A text-only quote of a photo post recorded `hasPhoto: true`, which fed the Playbook's media cell a photo the author never attached; it is now quote-scoped, and it **deliberately disagrees** with `tweetKind.ts`, which counts that same photo on purpose — the sweep gate asks *does this cell show an image*, the harvester asks *did the author attach one*, and only the second belongs in the media cell. And a browser locale past the English aria-label stems zeroed every metric on every harvested row; the reader now falls back to X's own testid buttons, in that order and not the other, because the label carries exact integers while the button carries a rounded, locale-formatted face. Both ship with fixtures — `extension/src/harvester.test.ts` is new, and is the first test file to sit directly in `extension/src/`.

  **$0, structurally.** No task in the wave could reach `xFetch` or `askLLM`: every read is over rows already stored, the scoring is arithmetic, and the estimator runs in the panel with no request per keystroke. Invariant #8 is untouched — nothing here adds a billed read back to get a count the DOM did not give us. **Deliberately not built:** a Playbook panel table for the cell (render it once it says something), Bangermeter's estimator table (`contentModifiers` would put a second rule vocabulary on the same sentence `postCoach` already grades — the suite asserts their ten ids are absent), their badge-on-every-article injection pass and breakdown panel, the 2023 verified boost and the community-note factor (one is archived code absent from the 2026 release, the other a round figure chosen inside a range from three studies — neither is an X parameter), a settings knob for any weight (a published weight is a transcribed fact, not a tunable), and any gate, sort or refusal driven by C or E. **The modifier magnitudes remain opening guesses**, each stamped `provenance: 'estimate'` with a ≥100-measured recalibration trigger; the risk they carry is that C *looks* authoritative because the weights are, and the pill copy is the only thing standing between those two facts. The weights themselves are synced from X's production by cron and can therefore change under us — `xRanker.ts` records the sync stamp, and a re-verification against a newer commit is a cheap future task that nothing auto-detects the need for. See `docs/composer-tab.md` §"The ranker score (C)", `docs/radar-tab.md` §"A row, part by part", `docs/voice-tab.md` §"What a save keeps besides the words", and `scripts/smoke-x-ranker.ts`.

- **Outliers — the X advanced-search compiler, clipboard hand-off, saved hunts (2026-08-25, `plans/2026-08-03-search-query-builder.md`, 8 tasks, $0):** goals 3 + 4. Until now the swipe file and the reply queue filled up from whatever crossed the timeline; finding material *deliberately* meant typing raw operator syntax into x.com's search box from memory, every session. The **Outliers** tab (Library rail, third — Voice · **Outliers** · Replies, panel tabs 14 → **15**) is a structured form — keyword groups all/any/phrase/exclude, from/to/@mentions/#hashtags, likes/reposts/replies floors, media/link/reply filters, language, date window, Top|Latest — that compiles to a correct x.com advanced-search string, shows it live with a character count against a **512** cap, and hands it off **Copy first** (paste into X's own search field) with **Open in X** second. Hunts are saved by name and re-load into the form intact.

  **The $0 is the decision, not a side effect — and it is invariant #8's opposite, made on the record.** X API v2 has **no** `min_faves` / `min_retweets` / `min_replies` operator at any access tier (verified against `docs.x.com/x-api/posts/search/integrate/operators`), so on the API "minimum engagement" could only be a client-side post-filter over `public_metrics` — and since X bills every result it *returns* (~$0.005 per other-user post), a `min_faves:400` hunt would pay for ~100 tweets to keep ~6. The web dialect supports the engagement operators natively for nothing. `searchRecent` was deleted on 2026-08-12 and stays deleted; nothing on any path in this feature can reach `xFetch` or `askLLM`, and `scripts/smoke-outliers.ts` therefore ships **no `--live` flag** because there is no paid claim a keyless run fails to make.

  **The results page needed no capture code at all.** `content.ts` attaches **Save to stratus** and the **⊕ Add to Radar** pin from one path-agnostic scan loop, so every search result already carries both — a search-results harvester would have duplicated `harvestClient.ts`'s port/scroll machinery for a page the existing per-tweet buttons already cover.

  **One dependency-free module owns the intelligence.** `src/shared/searchQuery.ts` is inlined into the panel through a re-export shim (the 12th out-of-tree `include`), so the form recompiles on every keystroke with **zero** network traffic and the previewed string can never disagree with what a save stores. It owns the rules that *are* the feature: the OR group is **always parenthesized**, even at one member (AND binds tighter than OR on X, so an unparenthesized group re-associates the moment another clause lands beside it), `OR` is uppercase or X matches the literal word, a `"` inside any term is an **error** rather than a silent strip (X has no escape character), `0` on a floor means *absent* rather than zero (a deliberate §7.11 carve-out — `min_faves:0` is inert noise eating the 512 budget), `replies`/`media` are enums because two booleans could say exclude-and-only at once while `hasLinks` gets both arms, dates must be real calendar dates, `lang:` is an **allowlist rather than a regex** (an unknown code returns zero results on X, which reads exactly like "no matches"), and a query with only floors and filters — the firehose minus a bit of it — is an error. **Problems are a list with two severities and only one of them refuses:** errors disable Copy/Open and block a write, warnings (a `:` inside a keyword, a multi-word OR member, a hashtag starting with a digit) never block anything. `searchUrl` returns `null` iff any problem is an error, so the type system carries the refuse-before-run gate.

  **Two load-bearing gotchas, both about X rather than about the code.** (1) **The web dialect is undocumented** — `docs.x.com` covers the API only, where these operators do not exist, so the operator set is a user-verified August 2026 snapshot re-checked against live x.com on 2026-08-24 by reading the returned posts' own like counts. (2) **A retired operator is *ignored*, not rejected** — the query silently returns the unfiltered firehose, and a broken feature then looks exactly like a working one. The symptom to report is *"suddenly far more results, at much lower engagement"*, and it is written into `docs/outliers-tab.md` for that reason.

  **Storage and routes.** `saved_searches` (migration **`0031_sharp_screwball`**, tables 43 → **44**) stores the **structured params**, normalized through `parseSearchQuery` on the way in, never the compiled string — a string can't be loaded back into a form, and a compiler fix then couldn't reach rows already saved. `query` is plain `text` and deliberately **not** `mode: 'json'`, so a row hand-edited out of band degrades *that row* to `compiled: null` and stays listed instead of throwing inside `.all()` and taking the whole list with it. `src/x/routes/searches.ts` is eight always-mounted $0 routes; `/searches/compile` and `/searches/defaults` register **above** `/searches/:id` (both are legal `:id` values). `sort` has exactly one authority and it is the column — the stored JSON is stripped of it going in and it is merged back coming out, resolving `body.sort ?? query.sort ?? x.outliers.sort`, never the column's `'live'` default, which exists only so the column can be notNull.

  **Six `scope: 'server'` knobs** (new group **Outliers**, registry 63 → **69 knobs / 15 → 16 groups**, mirrored unmoved at **33**) hold what a fresh hunt opens with: likes floor **400**, reposts and replies floors **0** (off), window **30** days, language **blank**, results tab **top**. The repo's first all-server group — the panel receives their *effect* through `GET /x/searches/defaults` and makes no client-side decision on the numbers, so §7.24's mirrored move never applied. **They are borrowed from a cheat sheet, not measured**, and that is exactly why they are knobs: the house rule is that a number is a measurement or it is absent, it is knowingly bent here because the user chose hand-tuning, and `docs/outliers-tab.md` labels them as borrowed. A ▲▼ stepper walks a fixed `FAVES_LADDER` (50…5000) whose bottom rung is `0` = "no floor", and the same rungs fill a `<datalist>` on all three floor boxes. **A computed outlier threshold was deliberately not built** — the recipe (P90 of `harvest_rows.likes` over 30 days for a topic hunt; that author's median likes × 3 at n≥20 for a profile hunt) is recorded in the plan's decision 10 as its own future plan, because the honest version needs a provenance line in the UI and a recalibration clause rather than a magic number. The follower-ratio variant was declined outright: its engagement-rate constant is undefendable and `account_snapshots` has been frozen since 2026-08-12, so "my follower count" is stale by construction.

  **The one measurement that did ship is the outcome, not the activity.** A run counter would have been activity nobody acts on; instead `voice_tweets.source = 'outlier_search'` answers the only question worth asking — did the swipe file actually fill up from this — and the tab renders the count as a footer line over a fixed 30-day window (deliberately *not* `x.outliers.sinceDays`: re-tuning how far back a hunt looks must not silently redefine what the counter underneath it measures). **The client reports WHERE, the server decides WHAT it means:** `content.ts` adds one field (`sourcePath: location.pathname`) and nothing else; `scrapeSourceFor` matches the path **segment**, not a prefix, because `/searchlight` is a claimable handle and `source` is insert-only. **First save wins** — `source` is deliberately absent from the scrape's `onConflictDoUpdate` set-clause, so a hunt-found tweet re-saved off the timeline keeps its provenance and vice versa. Zero renders as a sentence rather than hiding, because a zero here is the measurement.

  **Seeds, never hidden filters.** Active-channel chips append their keywords into the any-box (a keyword-less channel renders disabled *and* appends a visible `· no keywords` to its label — Chrome suppresses pointer events on a disabled control, so a tooltip alone would never have appeared), and a 2–10× target-roster picker types a handle into **From** while staying on its own placeholder, so a hand-typed handle is never contradicted. Nothing seeds itself. **New hunt re-reads `GET /x/searches/defaults`** rather than replaying the mount-time seed, which is what makes the tab's gear observable at all.

  **Deliberately not built:** any X API v2 search path (`dialect: 'api'`, a `public_metrics` post-filter, an "estimated spend" UI), a search-results scraper or `SEARCH_PORT`, widening `isHomeTimelinePath` so passive harvest runs on `/search`, an **MCP tool** (it would bump `src/mcp.test.ts`'s asserted **28** and three doc counts for a surface an agent has no reason to drive), scheduled/recurring hunts or result-diffing (all of which turn a $0 open-a-tab feature into a polling surface X's web search cannot serve), and the `point_radius:`/`place:`/`list:`/`url:` operator family. See `docs/outliers-tab.md`, `docs/settings-tab.md` §"The sixteen groups", `docs/voice-tab.md` §"Where the tweets come from", and `plans/2026-08-03-search-query-builder.md`.

- **OFF-PILLAR + REMIX — the drafter's default goes off-taxonomy, `reflective` becomes `bait`, and the remix gets a box (2026-08-23, ad-hoc/user-directed, no plan file, $0 — no schema, no migration, no new route):** three changes to the post/thread drafter, all in service of goal 1.

  **(1) "No pillar" is the Composer's default.** The Pillar dropdown gained a third static option above *any pillar (Grok declares)* — **No pillar (off-taxonomy)** — and it is what the tab opens on. It rides the wire as an ordinary `pillar` value, `NO_PILLAR = 'none'` (`src/x/posts/pillars.ts`), and the drafter's `parseCommon` turns it into an `offPillar` boolean rather than a slug. Off-pillar then means four things at once: the structured-output enum narrows to `[NO_PILLAR]`, the rendered taxonomy in `{{PILLARS}}` is replaced by the exported `OFF_PILLAR_BLOCK` (so the pillar bodies never enter the prompt and cannot steer the drafts), `{{PILLAR}}` renders `none`, and the inserted rows land with **`pillar: null`**. All three drafting routes honour it — `/posts/draft`, `/posts/reup`, and `/posts/draft-thread`, whose prompt gets the same block plus a steer line (`thread prompt.md` itself was not touched; the instruction rides the substituted block, so the byte-sync holds). **`parsePillar` was left alone** — it is shared with the calendar, articles and channels routes, and the sentinel is a drafter concept, not a taxonomy one. An active pillar that literally owns the slug `none` still wins, checked before the sentinel.

  **(2) The three registers are `plain | spicy | bait`.** `reflective` is retired. `spicy` was sharpened to *punchy* (contrarian hook, short hard lines, zero hedging), and `bait` is the answer-me register: it now owns §9's four **Proven engagement formats** outright, so GT.2's "at most ONE of the three drafts may use one of these" rotation cap became "the bait draft uses exactly one; plain and spicy use none". This is not the engagement-bait §7 forbids and the prompt says so in both places — a real question the account holder would answer in public, never "like if you agree". The measured basis is GT.2's, unchanged: on a comparable builder account, posts engineered to be *answered* took roughly 2x the views of posts engineered to be admired, so the third slot is always one of them instead of a narrative register.

  **What did not move.** `scheduled_posts.register` is free text and the Playbook reads it as such, so pre-2026-08-23 rows keep `register='reflective'` and their pillar × register cells still aggregate; `.badge-reflective` stays in `styles.css` for them, beside the new `.badge-bait`. `parsePostDrafts` degrades an unknown register to `plain`, so a stale `reflective` from a cached prefix cannot produce a malformed row. `CLAUDE.md` untouched — no guardrail moved.

  **(3) The *Tweet remix* box.** The Voice tab's **remix** button used to hand the Composer a bare tweet id and show a *"remixing structure of tweet …"* chip — the material itself was invisible, and the server only saw the saved row. The drafter now has a **Tweet remix** textarea: the remix click lands the tweet's text in it (with its extracted template, when it has one, as a note underneath), the box is editable, and a tweet pasted from anywhere works without a saved row. It rides the wire as **`remixText`** (≤2000, `400 invalid_remix_text`, validated before any spend) on `/posts/draft`, `/posts/reup` and `/posts/draft-thread`. Sent with `voiceTweetId` it wins over the row's stored text — the box is what the human was looking at, edits included.

  Two behaviours changed with it. `RemixSource.rawText` now ships **even when the template is extracted**: the extracted fields say what shape to borrow, the tweet says what that shape sounds like in the wild, and `renderRemix` emits both blocks. And the Composer **no longer clears the remix after a generate** — that auto-clear existed because the remix was invisible state that could silently apply to the next, unrelated draft; a visible box makes it wrong, since regenerating is the common case. The ✕, or emptying the box, clears it.

  **What keeps it from being a copy machine: prompt §10, `Remixing a tweet`.** Borrow the machinery only (hook shape, beat order, line-break rhythm, sentence-length pattern, length, closing device); take nothing off the surface — words, phrases, claims, numbers, names, examples, or the original's *topic*; the subject stays mine; loose, not traced (matching beat-for-beat reads as a knockoff); and the test the section ends on — *if the original author read my post, they should recognise the shape and not a single sentence*. §0–§3 and §7 outrank it, and where a borrowed shape fights a register the register wins. The thread prompt has no §10 (it is post §0–§5 only), so `buildThreadDraftInput` folds the box into the steer **with the anti-copy rule written inline** — if §10's wording moves, that block moves with it.

  **The trap this leaves.** The prompt registry (AI.3) can hold a **DB override** of the post prompt body, and `loadPromptSafe('post')` prefers it over the shipped template. An override saved before this change still describes the reflective register while the structured-output enum says `bait` — the model obeys the enum and reads contradicting prose. If the Prompts tab shows a post-prompt override, reset it (or re-apply the §4/§8/§9 edits by hand) after deploying.

- **RQ — The Radar's live queue: a dismissal is a fact the server holds too (2026-08-18, ad-hoc lane `plans/2026-08-18-radar-live-queue.md`, $0):** measured on 2026-08-18 over one `/home` session (97 sightings in 24 h), the side panel showed **Queue (35)** and `/radar-analyst` read **55**. The ~20-row gap was every tweet the operator had waved off with **✕** or **Clear** without ever drafting one: the dismissal lived only in `chrome.storage.local`, and the single thing the dismiss handler told the server — `markDraftsOnServer(ids, 'expired')` — PATCHes `radar_drafts`, a table with no row for a tweet that was never drafted. So a drafting session worked a different set of tweets than the human was looking at, and re-surfaced things already thrown away.

  **What shipped, four commits.** (1) `radar_sightings.dismissed_at` (nullable, migration `0030_famous_wrecker`, one `ALTER TABLE`, 16 → **17 columns**, no index — `SIGHTING_SCAN_CAP` already bounds the scan and this is a single-user DB) + **`PATCH /x/radar/sightings {tweetIds[1..200], dismissed: true}` → `{updated}`**, modelled line-for-line on `PATCH /x/radar/drafts`, plus one new rule in `mergeSightingRow`. (2) `background.ts`'s `isRadarDismiss` handler gained `markSightingsDismissedOnServer` beside `markDraftsOnServer` — the same fire-and-forget, 200-id-chunked, warn-and-drop shape, and one call site covers all four dismissal paths (the ✕, **Clear**, `consumeQueue`'s leftovers after a drafting pass, the curate pass's drops). (3) **`GET /x/radar/sightings?queue=true`** — the panel's predicate server-side: last seen inside `RADAR_QUEUE_TTL_MS`, `dismissed_at IS NULL`, not worked; `x_radar` gained the matching `queue` input. (4) `.claude/skills/radar-analyst/SKILL.md` reads the queue by default and treats a requested N as a **contract**: bait and age became rank penalties instead of pre-ranking drops, the one hard drop left is a row that cannot be answered without inventing something, and a shortfall is reported in one line naming that test rather than silently absorbed.

  **Three decisions worth not re-litigating.** `queue=true` is a **preset, not a fourth filter** — `days` is ignored and the response echoes the window actually used (`days: 1`), `worked` is forced false, and an explicit `worked=true` is a **400 `invalid_queue_combo`** raised before any DB work, because the empty set it would otherwise return reads as "your queue is clear". A dismissal is a **one-way ratchet**: the update carries `isNull(dismissed_at)`, so a re-dismiss honestly answers `updated: 0`, and the only thing that clears the stamp is a `manual`-band re-ingest — the ⊕ pin, the server mirror of the panel's `purgeDismissed`. A plain re-sighting deliberately does **not** resurrect a dismissed tweet, because the panel's tombstone does not either. And the queue TTL now exists **once**: `RADAR_QUEUE_TTL_MS` moved into `src/shared/radarSweep.ts` (still zero-import) with `extension/src/shared/radar.ts` re-exporting it as `RADAR_TTL_MS`, so no call site moved and the two sides cannot drift — this is the §7.33 "restate, never import" habit's opposite case, since the whole feature is the two sides agreeing on one number.

  **The caveat, stated because it is the shape that was chosen.** The mirror is an **event, not a snapshot** (§7.8 best-effort side writes): the PATCH is fired and forgotten, never retried, and never allowed to precede or block the buffer write the user is waiting on. A dropped request means one row drifts back into the server's view of the queue — the same failure every other best-effort side write in this repo already accepts — and the alternative, a snapshot PUT of the panel's id list, is exact by construction but needs a trigger and reads as authoritative while the panel is closed. Drift is repaired by the next dismissal of that row. **No new panel UI:** the `Queue (N)` badge the operator already reads is the contract, and a second "server sees N" badge would only expose the drift as a thing to worry about. `scripts/smoke-radar-access.ts` grew a **live queue** segment (three rows in → `queue=true` sees three → one PATCHed dismissal leaves the queue while the corpus keeps the row and says `dismissed: true` → a re-dismiss updates nothing → after a compose the queue is one row → a `manual` re-ingest puts the dismissed row back), $0 with no `--live`. **Owed:** the browser end-to-end — `x_radar({queue:true})` and the panel's badge agreeing on a live page — is `VERIFY-DEBT.md` item **`0x`**, for the same reason RA.8's `0w` is: a page tab cannot read another extension's `chrome.storage.local`, so it needs the human at the browser.

- **SW.1 — The sweep's two content gates: media and ads (2026-08-18, ad-hoc/user-directed, no plan file, $0, no schema, no migration):** an armed sweep can now filter on **what the tweet is**, not only on how it is doing. - **The LLM judge — a 13-dimension read on one draft, with anchored fixes and its own falsification cell (2026-07-27, $0 recurring, ~$0.003 per click):** the harvest of `evals/x-builder-judge-generate-analysis.md`, a code study of another builder's judge and generation engine. Full plan `plans/2026-07-22-llm-judge.md` (JD.1–JD.8, masterplan Wave 6 — the wave's last lane). One new table, two new prompt keys, **no worker and no automatic invocation anywhere**.

  **(a) Provenance hygiene, before anything paid (JD.1).** `topWinners` — the drafter's few-shot "this is your voice" block — selected own measured posts with no provenance filter, so a post the drafter wrote, that got scheduled, shipped and performed well, came back as a voice anchor with the measurement layer confirming the drift (study §G2). It is now capped at **2 of 5** drafter-authored rows. **Dilution, not exclusion** (a hand-edited machine draft is partly yours), and the cap is a cap: an all-machine history yields a *shorter* block, never five machine posts. The trap underneath it is worth remembering — `posts_published.source` is the PUBLISH path (`scheduled` | `manual`), not authorship; the only column that answers "who wrote this" is `scheduled_posts.source`, reached through `posted_tweet_id`. Both reply prompts also gained a one-line **trust label** above the quoted tweet (§G5), rendered into the *value* so the `.md` files and their TS literals stay byte-identical and the byte-sync tests don't move.

  **(b) The rubric (JD.2/JD.3).** `src/shared/judge.ts` (zero-dependency, extension shim) owns the vocabulary — thirteen dimensions, the band cut points in **one** place (`deriveVerdictBand`: ≥85 post it / ≥70 slight rework / ≥40 major rework / else do not post), `deriveApproved`, and `locateAnnotations`, the DOM-free quote locator that walks left-to-right from a consumed offset and **silently drops** a quote it cannot find. Its polarity list is the load-bearing export: `negativeRisk` 94 and `statusDependency` 94 are *bad*, and a panel colouring scores green-high would say the opposite. `src/x/judge/prompt.ts` owns the template, the schema and `parseVerdict`. **The schema carries no numeric bounds on purpose** — `minimum`/`maximum` are on the same strict-structured-outputs unsupported list as `minItems`, so shipping them risks a provider rejection on every call; the **parser** enforces 0–100 and the 12/5 caps. Generalized as codemap §7 rule **35**: what the schema cannot express, the parser enforces, and the parser is what a test pins.

  **(c) The two routes (JD.4/JD.5).** `POST /x/judge` (~$0.003) and `POST /x/judge/apply` (~$0.007), mounted behind the LLM gate beside the drafter. Four properties they exist to hold: **the verdict is derived, never model-supplied** (the schema omits it entirely, so a label and a number cannot disagree); **refuse before spend** — every validation, the 404, both 409s and the prompt loads land before `askLLM`; **a failed persist never loses a paid verdict** (the `draft_judgments` insert is best-effort and the route returns `id: null` rather than swallowing what you paid for); and **the returned verdict always describes the returned text**, held structurally by routing every exit through one payload builder that hashes the text it is handing back. The never-worse guard is strictly `>`: at temperature 0.2 a cosmetic rewrite ties far more often than it loses, so `>=` would quietly hand back a machine's rephrasing of your post on the strength of no evidence. `apply` is **two** calls where the source does three — persisting the verdict bought a call back.

  **(d) The panel (JD.6).** A `JudgePanel.tsx` under the single-post textarea — not inside `Composer.tsx`, which was already 1 406 lines and Wave 6's choke point; the judge needs three things from it (`text`, `setText`, a textarea ref), so Composer grew **13 lines**. Two behaviours worth stating: the reset-on-edit rule is **derived, not stored** (an effect would paint the stale verdict for a frame every time you type — and derivation means undo *restores* the verdict, because it genuinely describes that text again), and the apply handler reads the **live** textarea value rather than its render-time closure, because a `<textarea>` swap has no undo and the call takes ~7 s with the box still editable.

  **(e) The falsification cell, in the same phase as the tool (JD.7).** `judgeEffectiveness` in `GET /x/playbook` — own measured originals bucketed by the verdict the judge gave **that exact text**, gated n≥20, with `unjudged` as its own bucket and an `approved`/`rejected` split beside the four bands (a 2-way axis clears the gate at half the sample, which on a cell fed by an on-demand button is the difference between an answer and a decade). The join is a **read-time text hash** — no `judgment_id` column, no backfill — which is semantically right rather than a shortcut: a verdict is only valid for the text it judged, so a post edited after judging *should* read as unjudged. Whitespace is the one exception, by design. Expect `unjudged` to be ~100% for a long time and expect "insufficient data" for months; that is the honest alternative to quoting a thirteen-dimension score as if somebody had checked it.

  **What was deliberately not adopted, which is half the study's value.** **Auto-judging the drafter's three candidates** — the source can, because its LLM is a subscription CLI at $0 marginal cost; here it would turn a $0.006 draft into ~$0.016 for verdicts on two drafts you will discard. **Judging replies** — $3–6/month on the surface with the least headroom, and the source's own reply policy sets `forbidJudge: true`, which is independent confirmation rather than a budget excuse. **Any reach forecast from judged quality** — four constants marked `// CALIBRATE` in the source. **Their knowledge base** (~700 posts from 16 other accounts whose stated core finding contradicts the active niche's persona) and **their 1 636-line voice-RAG embedder**. And **no provider picker in the panel**, despite the plan's own line: Settings → AI owns the provider, and on the apply path an override steers only the *writer* while the re-judge stays pinned to the stored grader — a control that changes half of what its label implies is worse than no control.

  **Verification:** `scripts/smoke-judge.ts` — **$0 by default**, rerunnable, real DB, namespace-then-delete (890-prefixed ids, every seeded `posts_published` row `retired: true`, judgments deleted by the hash of their own fixture text). It drives the pre-spend refusal ladder on both routes, then the assertion the file is built around: a **valid** body reaching `503 llm_not_configured` with both provider keys force-unset, which is the only status that says everything before the money passed. The apply guards are asserted by *which* refusal fires — a double space must land on `nothing_to_apply` and never on `stale_verdict`, or reflowing a draft would silently cost you the verdict you paid for. The Playbook cell is proven as **deltas against a baseline read before the first seed** (the table now has three readers), including the newest-verdict-per-hash rule with explicit timestamps, a published row whose stray double space still matches its judgment, a `surface='reply'` judgment that hashes to a published original and is still excluded, and the gate moved to `minN=1000` where every cell goes insufficient and all five spreads go null. `--live` adds **exactly one** real judge call (~$0.003) — the only way to verify that the output-token cap is big enough for the provider to close the JSON, that all thirteen dimensions survive the parser, and that the persisted `text_hash` is the one the Playbook probes.

Its snapshot/restore carries a sentinel the writes are tagged with: a run killed mid-way leaves *its* config in the one row there is, and finding the sentinel at entry means the baseline is unrecoverable and the honest recovery is a reset, not adopting it.

- **Radar curated drafting — score the queue, dismiss the noise, draft the top N (2026-07-28, $0 recurring; ~$0.003–$0.015 per "Curate & draft" click, on top of the batch draft it protects):** the Radar's one paid drafting click used to spend on the newest 20 of a 40+ row queue, filler included, because ranking sees heat and relationships but not whether a post is a "drop a link, let's connect" thread. Now a cheap text-only scoring call goes in front of it. Full plan `plans/2026-07-28-radar-curated-drafting.md` (RC.1–RC.5, masterplan Wave 7). **One column, one prompt key, one knob, one route — no new table, no X API call, no MCP tool (23 unchanged), and the drafting call itself is byte-untouched on its happy path.**

  **(a) The pure core, and why its parser is strict in three places and lenient in one (RC.1).** New `src/x/replies/curate.ts` — `CURATE_PROMPT_TEMPLATE` (registry key **`reply-curate`**, prompt keys 15 → 16, TS-only like `reply-batch`: no `.md` twin, no byte-sync test), `CURATE_SCHEMA`, `buildCurateInput`, `parseCurateScores`, `selectCurated`, `MAX_CURATE_TWEETS = 100` (the radar ring buffer — the panel can never hold more). Pure by construction: no db, no settings store, no route import, because the prompt registry imports the template from here and a back-import cycles. `parseCurateScores` is strict on `id`/`score`/`lowValue` and degrades the **whole response** to null if any of the three is off-contract — a defaulted `lowValue` would dismiss a tweet nobody flagged, and a fractional score means the model ignored an `integer` schema, so its keep/drop judgement is not trusted either. It is lenient on `reason` (missing → `''`, over-long → clipped at 120): refusing over display metadata throws away a call already billed. `selectCurated` is anchored to the **asked** ids, iterating them rather than the response, which makes the tie-break the panel's own ranked order and makes a verdict on an id nobody asked about inert. `keep` comes back **best-first**, which is what lets a later trim drop the weakest survivors rather than an arbitrary one.

Nothing about it reaches the model: `renderBatchTweet` builds from four named fields and the confirm endpoint reads named columns, so neither spreads a row and neither can be made to leak the score by a later task forgetting.

It loads no relationship brief, no guidance, no me-context and no pillars, because none of it changes whether a post is worth replying to and every skipped lookup is latency on a click someone is waiting behind. A parse failure after the call is a **502, never a retry** — a retry doubles the spend on a model that just proved it is answering off-contract. The response is `{scored, keep, drop, unscored, keepTarget, costUsd, model, requestId}` and the route persists **nothing**: the queue is the extension's session ring buffer, so only the panel can act on `drop`, and the score reaches the DB one call later.   **(d) The panel orchestrates, and the order is the contract (RC.4).** A second header button, **Curate & draft (N)**, rendered only when the fresh queue outgrows the effective size — below that the plain button already covers everything and grading is a second call that changes nothing. One click: partition (⊕ pins aside), grade the rest, dismiss `drop`, draft pins-first + survivors trimmed to the batch cap, report both calls as one price. **Nothing is dismissed until the grade has answered** — refuse-before-drop, the client-side twin of §7.4 — and ids the model never scored are neither drafted nor dismissed, so a truncated answer costs coverage, never queue rows. A **blank-text row is skipped by both halves**, including a pin: `parseBatchTweets` rejects an empty `text` with a *whole-request* 400, and widening what we send from the top 20 to the entire queue is what surfaces that — one image-only sighting would otherwise 400 a 40-tweet pass with an error naming an index the user can't see. Both calls report failure as a **return value** rather than setting the note themselves, because a note written inside the drafting call could only ever describe half the click. Every early exit still prints the grading spend (`… · nothing left to draft · $0.0051`), and `· U unscored` appears only when U > 0 — a queue that scored 38 of 42 must not read as "42 was all there was".

The fix is one filter; widening an extension task's blast radius into the older button's untested happy path at the end of a lane is not how it should land.

- **Thread harvest — capture a whole conversation, then chat with it (2026-08-14, $0: nothing on any path in this feature can reach `xFetch` or `askLLM`):** goal 3. Full plan `plans/2026-08-14-thread-harvest.md` (TH.1–TH.7), shipped whole. **No new table, no worker, no X call, no LLM call, no registry knob, no prompt key.** One nullable column (migration `0028`), three routes, two MCP tools, one on-page button, one panel section.

  **What it does.** Open a thread on x.com and the root tweet's action row carries a **🧵** button — the fifth control in the UI.18 cluster (`ACT_ORDER.thread = 5`), and it appears only where `findPermalink(article).tweetId === focusedTweetId`, so never on a reply's own row. One click scrolls the conversation, clicks through "Show more replies", and saves the root plus every top-level reply — text, author, timestamp, replies/reposts/likes/bookmarks/views, media flags — as one capture. **The side panel does not need to be open:** the whole run lives in the content script, the label counts up in place (`Capturing… 47`) and settles on `Saved 143`.   **(a) One nullable column, no new table (TH.1).** Thread rows reuse `harvest_runs`/`harvest_rows` behind a free-text `mode = 'thread'` — the HV.1 ambient-ingest pattern — identified by the new nullable **`harvest_runs.root_tweet_id`** + index `(root_tweet_id, created_at)` (migration `0028_glorious_chat`, `ALTER TABLE` + `CREATE INDEX`, inspected, no seed dropped; **the next migration owns `0029`**). Root row is `groupPosition = 0` with null `orig*`; reply *k* is `groupPosition = k+1` with `orig*` = **the root** (the only widening is that "the tweet replied to" now means the thread root — the one parent X's DOM states reliably). **`POST /x/harvest/thread {root, replies[]}` is one atomic call**, capped at 500 rows: root and replies are meaningless apart and the client has all of them when the scroll ends. Validation is fully pre-write and ordered (§7.4 even though nothing here is billed) — the 501-row refusal fires **before a single reply is parsed**. The server owns three things the client may not assert (§7.16): `groupPosition`, each reply's `orig` (stamped from the *parsed* root; a client `orig` is ignored), and the run row — and **`MODES` still excludes `'thread'`**, so `POST /harvest/runs` keeps rejecting it and no client can forge a thread run (HV.1 decision 7). Duplicates — a virtualized re-sighting, or the root rendering inside its own conversation — are dropped first-wins and **counted** into `skippedDuplicate`. **No reply-draft reconcile and no person events**, both deliberate: OH.2 measured 0 of the first 98 harvested replies matching a draft row, and reading a thread is exposure, not interaction (HV.1 decision 6). Re-capture **appends** a new run — the longitudinal contract `harvest_rows` has always had.

  **(c) Two reads, one of them the point (TH.2).** `GET /x/harvest/threads?limit=` lists one entry per root — its **latest** capture, newest first, with `captures` counting how many times that thread has been captured — off a bounded `MAX_THREAD_RUN_SCAN = 500` newest-first scan grouped in TS, then ONE `inArray` select of the `groupPosition = 0` rows (early-return on empty, never an `in ()`). `GET /x/harvest/threads/:rootTweetId?runId=` returns the transcript: root + replies ordered by `position`, each with capture-time metrics and a read-time **`isAuthor`** (`row.handle === root.handle`, derived not stored — §7.12), plus `captures[]`, the axis `?runId=` reads along. **This is `routes/harvest.ts`'s first `:param` route**, so §7.20 stopped being a "keep it that way" note and became a live trap: the static `/harvest/threads` is registered immediately before, and adjacent to, the param one. **Completeness is reported, never stored** — the root's `comments` (what X claims, including nested and deleted replies) beside `replyCount` (what was actually scraped). Those two numbers side by side beat a client-asserted `complete` boolean, which was never going to be true and would have cost a column to be wrong in.

  **(d) The extension, in three layers (TH.3–TH.5).** `extension/src/shared/thread.ts` is the pure, bun-tested half — `threadRootIdFromUrl`, `extractedToIngestRow`, `dedupeThreadReplies`, `MAX_THREAD_REPLIES = 400` — with **zero value imports** (only `import type`, both erased under `verbatimModuleSyntax`), because it is inlined into the content-script IIFE (§7.26); `postsIngestRows` was deliberately **not** refactored to call it, so the shipped harvest path stays byte-untouched. `harvester.ts::captureThread` is a sibling of `runHarvest`, not a mode of it: it **takes the same module-level `running` flag**, which is what makes passive capture suspend for the duration and a hand-run harvest refuse with `already_running`, released in a `finally` on every exit path. Its store is `Map<tweetId, {row, order}>` with `order` fixed at **first sighting** (X virtualizes; a recycled article must not renumber) while re-sighting overwrites the row with fresher metrics, and `revealOriginals()` runs **per sweep** (TR.1's reason verbatim). `content.ts` adds the button, whose own `data-state` is the double-click lock and whose failures read as words (`Harvest running` / `Not a thread` / `Root not found` / `Failed`, with `unconfigured` reverting **silently** — nothing was lost, there was just nowhere to send it). The Harvest tab's **Captured threads** section (TH.6) renders the list with the `passiveRows` discipline: **null renders nothing, never a fabricated "0 threads"**.

- **Radar access — the swept queue, readable and draftable from a Claude Code session (2026-08-17 → 2026-08-18, $0 recurring and $0 per call; it can only *reduce* spend):** goals 2 + 4. Full plan `plans/2026-08-17-radar-access.md` (RA.1–RA.8), shipped whole; masterplan **Wave 8**, which closes the masterplan at **149/149**. One new table (migration `0029`), four routes, three MCP tools (25 → **28**), one extension ship hook, one panel button, one skill. **No `xFetch`, no `askLLM`, no image call on any path** — reply drafting moves off Grok onto the operator's own Claude Code session.

  **What it does.** A swept tweet used to exist only in the browser (`chrome.storage.local`, 500 rows, 24 h TTL) unless a Grok batch draft happened to persist it — so a terminal session could read what was *drafted* and what /home fed you three weeks ago, but never what your armed sweep actually admitted, and never a sweep on `/search`, a list or a profile. Now every sighting the Radar queues is mirrored to the server at capture time **with the page it came from**. From a session: `x_radar` answers "what did my sweep admit in the last N days, which of it is unworked, and which of it still clears today's filters"; `x_radar_tweet` shows one tweet's whole history (sightings → drafts → posted reply); `x_radar_draft_reply` composes 2–3 angle variants straight into the Radar queue; one **Fetch drafts** click in the panel makes those rows reply-ready — same card, same angle tabs, same click-to-copy — with the paste still manual (invariant #2).

  **(a) One upserted table, not a second series (RA.1).** `radar_sightings` is keyed by `tweet_id` and upserted: `harvest_rows` already owns the longitudinal curve for /home, and this table answers "what did my sweep admit and what happened to it", so bounded growth beat a second series (≤2,000 new rows/UTC-day × 60-day retention). `bandStickiness` + the `RadarBandName` union moved out of the extension into `src/shared/radarSweep.ts` so the page and the server ratchet a band with **one** copy of the rule (§7.27); the extension keeps `RadarBand` as an alias, so no importer changed. Merge rules are §7.9 fill-only for `first_seen_at`/`posted_at`/`source_path`/`verified`, max for `last_seen_at`, and **a freshness tie-break of `>=`, not `>`** — a band change punches through the 60 s recapture throttle, so the second POST can land in the *same millisecond* as the first, and a strict `>` stored nothing but a bumped `seen_count`.

  **(b) The client refuses rows the server would 400 on (RA.2).** `background.ts::addSightings` gained a best-effort `shipSightings` — the `isRadarReport` branch's success arm, so it covers sweep, cannon, roster **and** ⊕ pins, and `sender.tab.url` gives `source_path` with zero content-script churn. The route parses every row before writing any and **400s the whole batch on its first bad row**; the client is warn-and-drop and never retries. Composed, one unrepresentable row would silently cost up to 99 good ones — so `toSightingWireRow` drops the row client-side instead, restating the four ceilings in `extension/src/shared/radarIngest.ts` (the extension may not import server modules, §5). The opt-out is a `chrome.storage.local` toggle (*Mirror Radar sightings to stratus*, default on) rather than a registry knob — the HV.2 precedent: a mirrored knob is seven edits and two exact-list tests for a switch only the human at the browser needs.

  **(c) `admitted` is recomputed, never stored (RA.3).** Every read runs `passesSweep` against `sweepConfigFromSettings()` and **echoes the config it judged with**, because a preset change legitimately re-reads history and the echo is what makes that legible rather than mysterious. It is judged at the age the row had when it was **last seen** — judging at `now` would make every row older than `maxAgeMin` read false and say nothing about anything but the last hour — and, unlike the Playbook's timeline funnel, it does *not* force `verifiedOnly` off, because `verified` is stored here, so the verdict is exact rather than approximate. Both sighting GETs write **nothing** (the `GET /radar/placed-today` property): an agent paging the corpus must not advance the panel's draft statuses. `admitted`/`worked` get their own 400 codes rather than the repo's lenient `=== 'true'` — **a lenient boolean is fine for a flag, never for a filter**, since `admitted=1` reading as "no filter" is a wrong answer, not a missing one.

  **(d) A composed draft is band + signals stamped server-side or it does not exist (RA.4).** `extension/src/shared/radar.ts::draftRowToSighting` returns `null` when either is missing, so a draft row without them is silently dropped on rehydrate — green tests, empty panel. `POST /radar/drafts/compose` therefore 404s an un-sighted tweet rather than writing a draft with null stamps, recomputes `signals.ageMin` at compose time (the confirm route derives the source post time back out as `draftedAt − ageMin`, so a draft written six hours after the last sighting would place the post six hours late), and expires prior `ready` rows for that tweet in the same txn — `radar_drafts` now has **two** writers racing the panel's "newest ready row wins". `model` is server-stamped **`claude-code-mcp`**: the confirm route copies it onto `reply_drafts.model`, which is the entire measurement apparatus for "do Claude-drafted replies out-earn Grok batch ones" (read at n≥20 per side, §7.19).

  **(e) The button, and the bug the plan could not have known about (RA.5).** `Radar.tsx` grew a **Fetch drafts** action reusing the existing `stratus/radar-rehydrate` message. The plan said no background change was needed — true of the wire, false of the behaviour: `rehydrateSightings` skipped every tweetId the buffer already held, which until RA.4 could not lose anything (the only writer of `radar_drafts` attached its replies on the way back). `x_radar_draft_reply` composes for tweets sitting in the **live** queue, so the whole row was dropped and the button would have answered `up to date` with the draft on the server. The skip now attaches `reply`/`variants` to a live row instead of replacing it — a capture-fresh row's signals still beat an hours-old draft's — and a row already carrying that text is left untouched **and uncounted**, so every pre-RA.4 path is a byte-identical no-op. **Generalize it: "no change needed in file X" is a claim about the code the plan read at writing time, and a lane that adds a second writer to a table invalidates every "this could only mean…" comment written when there was one.**

  **(f) A diagnosis, not a feature (RA.6).** The passive `/home` corpus had taken no rows since **2026-07-27**; the lane's own skill would otherwise have presented a 21-day hole as "what the algorithm feeds me".
