# 12 connect posts — ready to publish by hand, Sep 7 → Sep 20 2026

**Use:** one every other day, manual paste. Not scheduled, not queued — these live outside the weekly set.
**Format:** no links (invariant #1 — a URL bills $0.20 vs $0.015), no hashtags, no media. Every proposition on its own line. All ≤280 chars.
**Persona facts used:** 51, veteran dev, full AI mode, one startup a month in public.

---

## The brief these are built to

Each post does **three** jobs at once, in this order of audience size:

1. **The majority — people already following you.** They've read your bio; they don't need to be told what you build. They get the real ask: **drop your status and what's blocking you** — plus a sincere line wishing them well. That's the reply engine, and replies are what carry the post to anyone new.
2. **Strangers in the niche.** The domains still have to be legible or the right people can't self-identify: indie hackers, build-in-public, AI + agents, SaaS, mobile dev, marketing/growth, growth psychology.
3. **You.** The 51 / one-a-month cadence, stated as fact in passing — never as the headline.

## What changed from the source drafts

The domains stay. The **bulleted list** goes. All 20 variants you pasted render the niche as a six-line emoji column, which is the single most-copied shape on builder X and reads as template before anyone gets to the words. Every post below expresses the same domains **inline, in one breath**, in a different way each time:

- as a cadence line — *"one startup a month in public — SaaS, AI agents, mobile, growth, and the psychology of not quitting"*
- as your open browser tabs — *"a SaaS dashboard, an agent loop that won't terminate, an App Store review page, an ad account I'm scared of"*
- as the blockers themselves — *"Stripe. An agent that loops. App Store review. A landing page that won't convert. Your own head."*
- as the kinds of people — *"three decades in", "the head game nobody talks about"*

Same filtering power, none of the template smell. And the domain phrase is never the ask — the ask is always status + blocker.

**The warm close is not decoration.** It's the reason someone who's had a bad week answers honestly instead of scrolling. Every post ends by wishing them something specific and real: that the blocker moves, that the bug is shallow, that this is the week it clicks. No two closes are the same.

Post them in the order under "Rotation" — it alternates high-energy with low-key so the timeline doesn't read as one long campaign.

## Working them

The post is a quarter of the value. The replies are the rest.

- **Answer every reply inside 90 minutes**, and answer the *blocker*, not the person. "🔥" is worth nothing; one specific sentence about their stuck point is what turns a reply into a follow — and reply depth is what keeps the post alive past the first hour.
- **The blocker answers are CRM gold.** Posts 3, 6 and 9 produce rows concrete enough to write a Circles note from ("stuck on Stripe webhooks", "App Store rejected twice"). Stash those people and follow up unprompted in two weeks — that follow-up is the whole relationship play, and nobody else does it.
- **Never two in 48 hours.** These are asks. Back-to-back asks read as a campaign and the second underperforms.
- **Rotation:** 1 → 3 → 11 → 6 → 4 → 9 → 8 → 2 → 5 → 12 → 10 → 7. Alternates energy; the two heaviest credential pushes (7, 8) sit far apart.
- **Track them as their own stream.** Different objective from the weekly set — replies and follows, not verified home-timeline impressions. When the own-profile harvest lands, bucket them separately before comparing anything to the scheduled 28.

## One caveat, stated once

The Aug 31 doc kept the connect shape out of the weekly set because it dilutes verified home-timeline share — broad low-quality engagement the payout formula doesn't reward. That still holds for the *scheduled* 28. These 12 are a deliberate exception with a different job: build the reply graph and the follow list. Keep the two sets apart and the objectives won't fight.
