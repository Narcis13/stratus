# Formats — the skeletons that print impressions, and how to remix one

Skeletons belong to everyone; subjects and phrases belong to whoever wrote
them. Borrow the machinery, never the surface. Every number below was counted
on a harvested corpus (`draft-posts/references/mechanics.md` has the full
tables); n and reply:like are stated so you can weigh them.

## 0. The one skeleton this account has already proven

> "Learn to code" was the advice of the 2010s.
> What's the equivalent advice for the 2030s?

~1.45M views, 6.4K likes, 2.7K replies, 512 quotes, on a 1.6K-follower
account (July 2026). 92 characters. Four properties, all load-bearing:

1. **A phrase the reader has said out loud.** Zero cost to form an answer.
2. **Pinned to a decade** (or a place, or a condition that already happened).
   That makes it civic, not personal.
3. **The successor fits in 3–8 words.** Every reply is a complete post, so
   replies get quoted, and quotes are what carry a post out of the graph.
4. **Nothing about the author in it.** No age, product, job.

The remixes (*learn to sell*, *learn to read*) did 16K–78K on small accounts,
which is why the seed under this shape is a **verdict**, not a question: the
remix you wish a smaller account would write, posted by you first.

Use it whenever the wave contains a slogan. Do not fire the July wording
again; swap the phrase and the pin.

## 1. The measured library

| Skeleton | Shape | Evidence | Two-second reply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Challenge board** | imperative + one-noun answer + latch: *Name one tool you'd miss. Not one you use, one you'd miss* | n=13, avg 27.7K views, r:l **1.84** (@danielsmidstrup) | a noun |
| **Referendum** | a slogan in quotes, forced to a binary: *"fake it till you make it" yes or no* | n=5, 119.6 comments vs 6.1 baseline, r:l **1.54** (@skpnky) | yes / no |
| **Receipts** | a live fad + a money ask that resolves to a number: *a hundred clones in a week did anyone make a dollar* | n=5, r:l **2.14**, max 15.7K (@skpnky) | a number |
| **Dilemma** | two options, both cost something, a latch that kills the third: *$40k now or bet on $400k in two years no third option* | n=17, 58 comments, r:l **1.15** (@i_mika_el) | A or B |
| **Faction split** | both tribes stated fairly, then "which are you": *half of you… the other half… which one is your September* | n=9, 42 comments, r:l 0.86 | a side |
| **Bake-off** | named options plus an escape slot: *cursor claude code codex or something else* | 135K-view max; brands recruit their own fanbases | a brand |
| **Word budget** | *in one word*, *two words max*, *talk me out of it* | 827 and 670 comments on n=2 | one or two words |
| **Gate** | title denial aimed at the reader + a number ask: *don't call it a startup until someone paid how many have* | gates run r:l ~0.9 on a 0.36 statement baseline (@charlesordo) | a number, or outrage |
| **Verdict** | expensive confession → binary on a contested identity, author in the dock | 53K on the best row, small n | agree / disagree |
| **Successor question** | §0 | 1.45M, own account | 3–8 words |
| **Tighter** | 2–4 short lines, one fact carries the credential, ends on the question | own-account form, in test | one to three words |

What is **not** in the library and stays out: link farms and "introduce
yourself" (r:l 1.78 but the repliers came to drop URLs and dilute the verified
share), milestone posts (avg 1.5K on n=25; from a 1.3K account reads as
begging), news commentary with no ask (0.2–0.8% engagement, the measured
worst), product announcements (the single worst post per view in the corpus),
borrowed catchphrases (*I'll wait*, *ok serious question*).

## 2. Picking the skeleton for the wave

| The wave contains… | Use |
|---|---|
| a slogan people repeat | referendum, or successor question |
| a fad everybody touched this week | receipts |
| a tool or vendor fight | bake-off, or dilemma with a money constraint |
| two tribes already arguing | faction split, or dilemma |
| a job title under attack | gate, or successor question |
| a thing people can enumerate | challenge board, or word budget |
| something you can confess to at a cost | verdict, or tighter |

Constraints that override the table:
- **Never the same skeleton as the last two originals** the account fired
  (Step 0 of the run shows them).
- **Never two `was the advice of` posts in one day**; the timeline punishes a
  trick it just saw.
- Challenge boards stay under 110 characters. Dilemmas need numbers inside
  the reader's reality ($40k, ten customers, six hundred hours; never $1.4M,
  that one ran r:l 0.28).

## 3. The remix rules

The draft must pass this test: **if the source author read it, they would
recognise the shape and not one sentence.**

- Borrow: hook shape, beat order, line-break rhythm, length, the closing
  device.
- Never carry over: a phrase, a claim, a number, an example, a name, or the
  source's angle on the subject.
- The wave's subject is fair game; the mover's subject is not. If the mover
  *is* the wave (one post started it), take the subject only after you have a
  twist the mover did not have.
- One twist per post (research.md §4). Two twists is an essay.
- The hole is deliberate: an absolute where an exception exists, a list that
  is missing the obvious entry, an option nobody can pick without cost, an
  unnamed answer. The replies exist to fill it.

## 4. The seed reply

Posted within 60 seconds, under your own post. It is the second surface.

Patterns that work, strongest first:

1. **Verdict** (for the successor question and referendum): *my answer
   learn to delete* + one line of reason. The remix a smaller account would
   have written.
2. **Challenge the credibility of one group of repliers**, never their
   opinion: *everyone about to answer "the junior" id like to know if you hired
   one this year*. Restarts the thread among people who already replied.
3. **Disqualify yourself first**: *ive been offered nothing so my answer is
   worth what youd expect*. Makes the challenge in the same seed land as fair.
4. **Reframe onto a different object**: turn a tool question into a question
   about the person you depend on.
5. **Falsifiable prediction**: *never once seen the price go down after*.
6. **Pre-load the obvious objection**: *someone is about to say humans did
   this for thirty years / correct*.
7. **Refuse to name your own answer**; half the follow-ups become guesses.

Anti-patterns: a seed that answers the question and closes it; a seed that is
a better aphorism than the post; a seed that agrees with the likely majority;
a seed that asks a second question (the post is the prompt, the seed is the
angle); a seed longer than the post.

## 5. Hook mechanics, for impressions specifically

- **First seven words carry it.** No preamble, no "unpopular opinion", no
  "hot take". Open on the sharpest noun or the phrase in quotes.
- **A claim with a hole beats a question with none.** *nobody has ever been
  promoted for deleting code* gets more replies than *should you delete code*.
- **A number is a hook only if the reader can picture being offered it.**
- **Pin it.** A decade, a place, a day of the week, *this September*. Pinned
  posts read as civic; unpinned ones read as opinion.
- **Short is a feature.** Under 110 characters the whole post is the hook,
  it renders fully on every surface, and ≥50% visibility for the qualified
  impression is trivially met.
- **Dwell comes from the double-read.** A line the reader goes back to check
  (*wait, is that true*) is worth more than a line they agree with.
- **Quotes carry a post out of the graph.** Write the line you would want
  quoted with three words in front of it.
